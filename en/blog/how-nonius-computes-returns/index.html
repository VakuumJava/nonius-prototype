How Nonius computes your returns

The methods behind every figure in Nonius: daily time-weighted return, money-weighted return, currency effect, net dividends and the benchmark.

A return figure is only as useful as your ability to check it. This note sets out how Nonius arrives at the numbers on your dashboard, so that you can reproduce them from your own records and know exactly what each one includes.

The time-weighted return, chained daily

The headline performance figure is a time-weighted return. Nonius values the whole portfolio at the close of every business day, in your base currency. For each day it computes a daily return: the day's value minus any external flow on that day, divided by the previous day's value, minus one. The daily returns are then chained by multiplying them together, so the figure for any range is simply the product of the days inside it. Days without trading carry the last available price, so a holiday never creates a return of its own.

External flows are deposits into the portfolio and withdrawals out of it. They are removed from the calculation, which is why a large transfer never shows up as a good day. Dividends and fees are not external flows: they happen inside the portfolio, so a dividend raises the return, and a custody fee or a trading commission lowers it.

The money-weighted return, alongside it

The Analytics view also shows a money-weighted return: the internal rate of return of your deposits and withdrawals, with the current value as the final amount. Where the time-weighted figure describes the investments, the money-weighted figure describes your own result, including when you added money. We show both side by side and do not blend them into one number.

Currency, income and the benchmark

Every position is converted into your base currency, EUR, USD, GBP or CHF, at that day's exchange rate. For holdings priced in another currency, Nonius splits the change in value into a price effect, measured at the starting exchange rate, and a currency effect, which is the rest. Both are reported on their own lines, per position and in total.

Dividends are recorded net of withholding tax, as the amount that actually arrives in your account. If a broker reports the gross amount and the tax separately, both are kept in the ledger, but the return uses the net figure.

The benchmark is the MSCI ACWI index (net, in EUR). It is compared over exactly the same dates as your portfolio, starting from your first deposit or from the start of the range you choose.

Every figure in Nonius can be rebuilt from your own transactions and the daily prices.

What Nonius does not do

  • It does not calculate tax. Net dividends are what you received, not what you owe; your tax adviser or tax authority has the final word.
  • It does not give advice. The figures describe what happened, not what you should buy or sell.
  • It does not forecast. There are no projected returns, only the record of the past.

If a figure looks wrong, the cause is almost always a missing or duplicated transaction. The transaction ledger shows every input the calculation used, and you can export it as CSV to check the arithmetic in a spreadsheet of your own.
